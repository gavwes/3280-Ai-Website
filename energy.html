Energy & Carbon

AI is one of the fastest-growing power users on the grid.

Data centers already use as much electricity as entire countries. AI is pushing that number up faster than utilities can build new power plants, and much of the new power still comes from fossil fuels.

Why AI is so power-hungry

Training is big. Everyday use is bigger.

Training a large AI model means running thousands of specialized chips (GPUs) nonstop for weeks or months. That is a huge one-time energy cost.

Inference is what happens every time someone uses the model: each prompt, each generated image, each AI summary in a search result. One response uses a small amount of energy, but it happens billions of times a day. As AI gets built into search engines, phones, email and study apps, inference becomes the bigger share of the footprint.

Inside a data center, most of the electricity goes to the servers themselves. A large share also goes to cooling, since nearly every watt a chip uses turns into heat that has to be removed.

How fast it's growing

Demand is on track to double

The International Energy Agency projects that data center electricity use will more than double by 2030, with AI as the main driver. In the United States, data centers' share of electricity could nearly triple in just five years.

The carbon connection

Clean energy can't keep up, so fossil fuels fill the gap

The climate impact of AI depends on where the electricity comes from. A data center running on wind and solar has a much smaller footprint than one running on coal or natural gas.

24/7

Data centers need power around the clock. Utilities often meet that demand with natural gas plants, because sun and wind aren't always available.

Delayed

Some utilities have delayed retiring fossil fuel plants or proposed new gas plants to keep up with new data center demand.

Rising

Several major tech companies have reported that their total emissions went up in recent years as they expanded AI, despite their climate pledges.

The key point: the same AI prompt can have a very different carbon footprint depending on when and where it runs. That's why our call to action includes pushing companies and universities to power data centers with clean energy and to be transparent about it.

"The cloud" isn't in the sky. It's a building plugged into your power grid.
